The domestication of the cat can be traced back to Near Eastern and Egyptian populations of the African wildcat, Felis lybica, from which emerged the domestic cat (Felis catus). Both belong to the Felis lineage of the family Felidae, which is thought to have first diversified around 13 to 12 million years ago. Several investigations have indicated all domestic varieties of cats come from a single lineage of F. lybica, although early interactions between wildcats and Neolithic human settlements occurred across a broad geographic range.
Early domestic cats followed farmers, with archaeological evidence indicating they had spread to the isolated island of Cyprus approximately 9,500 years ago and as far as Bulgaria and Romania 6,000 years ago.
